SPF flattening: what can go wrong, and how to do it safely

Flattening means replacing include: mechanisms with the ip4: and ip6: ranges they currently resolve to. IP mechanisms cost no DNS lookups, so a flattened record easily fits under the limit of 10.

The risks

  • Stale IPs. Providers change their sending ranges without telling you. A static flattened record keeps the old list, and mail from the new IPs fails SPF. This is the main risk.
  • Length. A single TXT string is limited to 255 characters. Longer records must be split into several strings, and a very long record can push DNS responses past 512 bytes. Splitting into chained records (include:_spf1.yourdomain.com, ...) avoids that, and costs one lookup per chained record.
  • Macros and exists:. Some providers (Salesforce, for example) use exists:%{i}.... These can't be flattened and have to stay as they are.
  • Qualifiers. Only pass (+) mechanisms should be flattened. Keep -include: or ~include: as-is.

Doing it safely

  1. Run node spf-check.mjs yourdomain.com --flatten with the free checker to get a preview, split automatically if it's long.
  2. Keep a copy of your original record.
  3. Re-check on a schedule (daily at least) and update the record when the resolved ranges change. Open-source flatteners such as cfspflat and sender_policy_flattener can do this on a cron job you run yourself.
